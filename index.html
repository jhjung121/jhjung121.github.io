Working Papers

  • Uneven Impacts of a New City on Neighboring Regions: Evidence from Sejong, Korea with Jun Sung Kim · revise and resubmit Conference Presentations: WEAI 99th Annual Conference, AASLE 2024 Conference
    Abstract

    We examine the geographical spillover effects of Sejong, a newly established administrative capital city in South Korea, on local labor markets in four neighboring counties, Cheonan, Cheongju, Gongju, and Yuseong. The establishment of Sejong represents a large-scale place-based policy that concentrated government institutions and population in a newly constructed city, potentially reshaping economic activity in surrounding regions. Using county-level panel data and the synthetic control method, we construct county-specific counterfactuals and analyze employment changes in the Accommodation and Food Service Activities industry, a non-tradable sector sensitive to local demand. We find heterogeneous spillover effects across neighboring counties. Employment increases in Cheonan and Yuseong, rises modestly in Cheongju, and declines in Gongju. Further analysis by worker type shows that most employment adjustments occur through non-regular employment and business ownership, while regular employment responds more unevenly across counties. These findings suggest that large-scale spatial interventions can generate both positive demand spillovers and adverse agglomeration shadow effects depending on local characteristics and spatial connectivity.
