Working Papers

  • Uneven Impacts of a New City on Neighboring Service-Sector Employment: Evidence from Sejong, Korea with Jun Sung Kim · revise and resubmit Conference Presentations: WEAI 99th Annual Conference, AASLE 2024 Conference, 2025 KEA Annual Meeting
    Abstract

    We examine the spillover effects of Sejong, a newly established administrative city in South Korea, on employment in neighboring service industries. Sejong concentrated government institutions and population in a newly constructed city, potentially reshaping local demand. Using county-level panel data for 2000–2019 and county-specific synthetic controls, we study employment in two major service industries across four neighboring counties: Cheonan, Cheongju, Gongju, and Yuseong. Estimates for Accommodation and Food Service Activities employment are positive in Cheonan, Cheongju, and Yuseong and negative in Gongju. Pointwise prediction intervals indicate statistically significant positive effects in Cheonan across post-intervention years, while statistical significance varies across the other counties. Estimates for Wholesale and Retail Trade employment reveal further heterogeneity: Cheonan shows a positive response with relatively stronger statistical support, while Yuseong shows a negative estimate. Estimates by worker type suggest differing responses among regular employees, non-regular employees, and business owners. These findings highlight the importance of examining localized service-sector responses to large-scale place-based policies.
